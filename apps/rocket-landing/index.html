Rocket Landing

The rocket doesn't follow a stored path. It solves a fuel-optimization problem again and again on the way down. Drag it, or the tip of its velocity arrow, then fly.

Simplified teaching model: a 2-D point mass with no aerodynamics. Not SpaceX flight software.

    How this works

    An engine that can't throttle to zero makes the allowed thrust a ring, which is not a convex set. Lossless convexification fills in the ring with one extra variable and proves the best answer doesn't change, so the plan can be computed reliably in a fraction of a second.

    The chart shows the result: full thrust, then minimum thrust, then full thrust. The plan keeps 10% of the thrust range in reserve for corrections.

    On Earth, aerodynamic forces dominate much of a real descent, so the real problem is harder than this one. Vehicle numbers here are illustrative.

    Sources: Blackmore, Autonomous Precision Landing of Space Rockets; Blackmore, Açikmeşe & Scharf, Minimum-Landing-Error Powered-Descent Guidance.
