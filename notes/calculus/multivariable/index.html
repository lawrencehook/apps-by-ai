Gradients, Hessians & change of variables

Local geometry is encoded by first and second derivatives.

Before this Derivatives & Taylor expansion, Eigenvalues & diagonalization

DefinitionsTheoremsApplicationsExercises 2

Definitions

01

Gradient

For a differentiable scalar function on Euclidean space, the vector representing its derivative under the Euclidean inner product.

02

Jacobian

The matrix of first partial derivatives of a differentiable vector-valued map.

03

Hessian

The matrix of second partial derivatives of a scalar function; symmetric when the second partials are continuous.

Key theorems

01

Second derivative test

At a critical point of a C² function, a positive-definite Hessian gives a strict local minimum, negative-definite a strict local maximum, and indefinite a saddle. Semidefinite cases are inconclusive.

02

Change of variables

For a C¹ diffeomorphism φ between open regions and an integrable function, integration transforms using |det Dφ| as the volume factor.

03

Lagrange multipliers

At a constrained local extremum of differentiable f under g(x)=c, if ∇g≠0, then ∇f=λ∇g for some λ.

Notable applications

01

Optimization

Gradients guide descent and Hessians describe local curvature.

02

Density transformations

The Jacobian determinant converts probability densities between coordinate systems.

Watch the assumptions

  • Existence of partial derivatives alone does not imply differentiability.
  • The Jacobian volume factor uses an absolute value; orientation is a separate issue.

Work it out

Try before revealing

01 / Classify

Classify the origin for f(x,y)=x²−y².

Hint

Find the Hessian eigenvalues.

Solution

∇f(0,0)=0 and H=diag(2,−2), so the origin is a saddle.

02 / Compute

Compute the area of a disk of radius R using polar coordinates.

Hint

The Jacobian factor is r.

Solution

Integrate ∫₀²π∫₀ᴿ r dr dθ=πR², including the Jacobian factor r.
