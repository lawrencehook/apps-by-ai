Estimation & likelihood

Choose a model, then distinguish an unknown parameter from a statistic computed on data.

Before this Random variables & moments

MSE(θ̂) = Var(θ̂) + Bias(θ̂)²
DefinitionsTheoremsApplicationsExercises 2

Definitions

01

Estimator

A statistic θ̂(X₁,…,Xₙ) used to estimate a parameter θ. Its sampling distribution describes its behavior under repeated samples.

02

Bias and consistency

Bias is Eθ[θ̂]−θ. Consistency means θ̂ₙ→θ in probability as n grows.

03

Likelihood

L(θ;x) is the joint mass or density at the observed data, viewed as a function of θ. Maximum likelihood chooses a maximizing parameter, if one exists.

Key theorems

01

Bias–variance decomposition

With finite second moments, mean squared error E[(θ̂−θ)²] equals variance plus squared bias.

02

Unbiased sample variance

For iid data with finite variance σ² and n≥2, s²=Σᵢ(Xᵢ−X̄)²/(n−1) satisfies E[s²]=σ².

Notable applications

01

Parameter fitting

Likelihood turns model assumptions into an optimization objective.

02

Regularization

Accepting some bias can reduce variance enough to improve prediction error.

Watch the assumptions

  • A likelihood is not a probability distribution over parameters.
  • Unbiasedness does not guarantee a small error, and a biased estimator can be consistent.

Work it out

Try before revealing

01 / Derive

Find the MLE of p from k successes in n independent Bernoulli trials, allowing p∈[0,1].

Hint

Maximize k log p+(n−k) log(1−p); handle endpoints separately.

Solution

For 0<k<n the derivative vanishes at p=k/n and the log-likelihood is strictly concave. If k=0 or n, the maximum is at 0 or 1. Thus p̂=k/n.

02 / Compute

Estimator A has variance 4 and bias 0; B has variance 1 and bias 1. Which has smaller MSE?

Hint

Apply the decomposition.

Solution

A has MSE 4; B has MSE 1+1²=2. B has smaller MSE despite its bias.
