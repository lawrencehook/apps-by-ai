Definitions

01

Direct proof

Assume the hypotheses and derive the conclusion.

02

Contradiction

Assume the negation of the desired claim and derive an impossibility.

03

Induction

Prove a base case and a step from a statement at n to the statement at n+1.

Key theorems

01

Induction principle

If P(0) holds and P(n) implies P(n+1) for every integer n≥0, then P(n) holds for every integer n≥0.

02

Well-ordering of ℕ

Every nonempty subset of the nonnegative integers has a least element; this supports minimal-counterexample arguments.

Notable applications

01

Algorithms

Loop invariants and induction establish that repeated operations preserve correctness.

02

Structure theorems

Induction on dimension or group order reduces large objects to smaller ones.

Watch the assumptions

  • The converse Q ⇒ P does not follow from P ⇒ Q.
  • To refute “for every,” one counterexample suffices; checking examples cannot prove an infinite universal claim.

Work it out

Try before revealing

01 / Prove

Prove 1+⋯+n=n(n+1)/2 for n≥1.

Hint

Add n+1 to the induction hypothesis.

Solution

The base case is 1=1. If the formula holds at n, the next sum is n(n+1)/2+(n+1)=(n+1)(n+2)/2.

02 / Find a counterexample

Is every continuous real function differentiable?

Hint

Look for a corner.

Solution

No. f(x)=|x| is continuous everywhere, but its left and right derivatives at 0 are −1 and 1.
