01

Foundations

The language of sets, maps, and proofs.

  • Sets & mapsSpecify the objects and the maps between them before manipulating either.↗
  • Proof techniquesChoose the logical shape of an argument before choosing its algebra.↗
02

Algebra

Equations, polynomials, and arithmetic structures.

  • Polynomials & rootsFactorization translates an equation into structural information about its roots.↗
  • Rings, ideals & fieldsTrack which arithmetic operations are available and which survive a quotient.↗
03

Linear algebra

Vector spaces, linear maps, and spectral structure.

  • Vector spaces & linear mapsSeparate coordinate-free objects from the matrices used to describe them.↗
  • Eigenvalues & diagonalizationFind directions on which a linear transformation acts only by scaling.↗
  • Orthogonality, least squares & SVDApproximate a vector by its closest point in a subspace.↗
04

Calculus & analysis

Limits, local change, accumulation, and approximation.

  • Limits & continuityMake approximation statements precise by controlling errors.↗
  • Derivatives & Taylor expansionThe derivative is a local linear model; Taylor expansion adds controlled higher-order terms.↗
  • Integration & accumulationIntegrals combine local contributions; the fundamental theorem links accumulation and change.↗
  • Gradients, Hessians & change of variablesLocal geometry is encoded by first and second derivatives.↗
05

Probability

Randomness, conditioning, and limiting behavior.

  • Events, conditioning & BayesUpdate probabilities by restricting the sample space and renormalizing.↗
  • Random variables & momentsA distribution describes a random quantity; moments summarize selected features.↗
  • LLN, CLT & concentrationUnderstand what averaging guarantees—and how much data a guarantee needs.↗
  • Modes of convergenceDistinguish pathwise behavior, small error probabilities, average error, and convergence of laws.↗
  • Conditional expectationAverage using the information available; least squares and conditioning are versions of the same projection idea.↗
06

Statistics

Learning from data while accounting for uncertainty.

  • Estimation & likelihoodChoose a model, then distinguish an unknown parameter from a statistic computed on data.↗
  • Confidence intervals & testsExpress sampling uncertainty without confusing the data with the hypothesis.↗
  • Linear regressionFit conditional means while keeping the assumptions behind inference visible.↗
07

Group theory

Symmetry, actions, and quotient structures.

  • Groups & subgroupsA group isolates the algebra of reversible operations.↗
  • Homomorphisms & quotientsA quotient identifies precisely the information a homomorphism forgets.↗
  • Group actions & countingStudy a group by what it moves and what it fixes.↗
08

Representation theory

Understand symmetry through linear transformations.

  • Representations & invariant subspacesReplace abstract symmetries by matrices that preserve composition.↗
  • Characters & decompositionTraces summarize finite-group representations without fixing a basis.↗
  • Direct sums, tensor products & dualsBuild new representations and track how their dimensions and characters change.↗
09

Computer science

Data, logic, and the correctness of programs.

  • Data structures & amortized costChoose a representation by the operations and guarantees the application needs.↗
  • Boolean logic, circuits & satisfiabilityConnect logical specifications to finite computations on bits.↗
  • Recursion, invariants & terminationA correct output and a guaranteed halt are separate obligations.↗
10

Algorithms

Design procedures, prove them correct, and bound their cost.

  • Asymptotics & recurrencesState the input-size measure and computational model before counting operations.↗
  • Sorting, binary search & lower boundsUse ordering to narrow possibilities, and distinguish algorithm cost from unavoidable cost.↗
  • Graph traversal & shortest pathsChoose a traversal based on edge weights, direction, and the question being asked.↗
  • Dynamic programming & greedy choiceIdentify what information a subproblem needs, or prove why a local choice is safe.↗
11

Theory of computation

Languages, machines, and the limits of computation.

  • Finite automata & regular languagesFinite memory recognizes patterns whose relevant history has only finitely many cases.↗
  • Context-free grammars & parsingA stack adds unbounded nesting to finite-state control.↗
  • Turing machines, deciders & recognizersDistinguish computing an answer eventually from guaranteeing an answer on every input.↗
  • Reductions & undecidabilityTransfer impossibility by showing how a hypothetical solver would solve a known impossible problem.↗
  • P, NP & completenessAsk what can be computed efficiently after establishing that it can be computed at all.↗
12

Real analysis

Completeness, convergence, and rigorous approximation.

  • Completeness & sequencesCompleteness guarantees that approximations whose internal error vanishes have a limit in the space.↗
  • Metric compactness & fixed pointsCompactness controls arbitrary covers; contraction controls successive approximations.↗
  • Series & uniform convergenceA pointwise limit answers one input at a time; a uniform limit controls all inputs at once.↗
13

Measure theory

Measure sets, integrate functions, and exchange limits safely.

  • σ-algebras & measuresNot every subset needs to be measurable; specify the collection on which size is defined.↗
  • Lebesgue integration & Lᵖ spacesBuild integrals from measurable simple functions, then identify functions that differ only on null sets.↗
  • Convergence theorems & iterated integralsBefore exchanging a limit or order of integration, identify the theorem whose hypotheses apply.↗
14

Complex analysis

Holomorphic functions, contour integrals, and residues.

  • Holomorphic functions & power seriesComplex differentiability in a neighborhood is far more rigid than real differentiability.↗
  • Contour integration & Cauchy formulasContour integrals connect local values of a holomorphic function to its behavior around a boundary.↗
  • Laurent series, poles & residuesThe coefficient of (z−a)⁻¹ records the local contribution to a contour integral.↗
15

Topology & fundamental groups

Continuity, deformation, and the algebra of loops.

  • Topological spaces & homotopyStudy properties preserved by continuous deformation rather than by a particular distance.↗
  • The fundamental groupEncode the ways a based loop can wind around holes.↗
  • Coverings & van KampenLift loops upstairs, or assemble a fundamental group from overlapping pieces.↗
16

Knots & braids

Embedded curves, crossings, and strand motions.

  • Knots, links & invariantsA knot is an embedding, not just a drawing with crossings.↗
  • Braid groups & closuresRecord strand motion while retaining information that a final permutation forgets.↗
17

Category theory

Objects through maps, naturality, and universal constructions.

  • Categories & morphismsDescribe structure through composable maps, without assuming the objects are sets with elements.↗
  • Functors, naturality & YonedaTransport structure between categories and compare those transports coherently.↗
  • Universal properties, products & adjunctionsSpecify an object by the maps it uniquely permits rather than by one construction.↗
