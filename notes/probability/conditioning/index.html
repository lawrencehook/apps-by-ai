Events, conditioning & Bayes

DefinitionsTheoremsApplicationsExercises 2

Definitions

01

Probability space

A triple (Ω,ℱ,P): outcomes, a σ-algebra of events, and a countably additive measure with P(Ω)=1.

02

Conditional probability

For P(B)>0, P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B).

03

Independence

A and B are independent if P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B). A family is mutually independent when the product rule holds for every finite subfamily.

Key theorems

01

Total probability

For a countable partition {Bᵢ} with positive probabilities, P(A)=ΣᵢP(A|Bᵢ)P(Bᵢ).

02

Bayes rule

The displayed identity follows by writing the joint probability in two ways; the denominator can be expanded by total probability.

Notable applications

01

Classification

Combine prior class probabilities with evidence likelihoods.

02

Reliability

Break a system into operating scenarios and combine conditional failure probabilities.

Watch the assumptions

  • P(A|B) and P(B|A) are generally different.
  • Disjoint positive-probability events cannot be independent.

Work it out

Try before revealing

01 / Compute

A factory uses machine A for 60% of parts and B for 40%. Defect rates are 1% and 3%. Given a defective part, what is the probability it came from B?

Hint

Compute the total defect rate first.

Solution

P(defect)=0.6·0.01+0.4·0.03=0.018. Thus P(B|defect)=0.012/0.018=2/3.

02 / Explain

For a fair die, are “even” and “greater than 3” independent?

Hint

Compare the joint probability with the product.

Solution

The joint event is {4,6}, with probability 1/3. The product is (1/2)(1/2)=1/4, so they are not independent.
