Conditional expectation

Average using the information available; least squares and conditioning are versions of the same projection idea.

Before this Events, conditioning & Bayes, Lebesgue integration & Lᵖ spaces, Orthogonality, least squares & SVD

E[E[X | 𝒢]] = E[X]
DefinitionsTheoremsApplicationsExercises 2

Definitions

01

Information

A sub-σ-algebra 𝒢⊆ℱ records which events are observable. E[X|Y] abbreviates E[X|σ(Y)].

02

Conditional expectation

For integrable real X, E[X|𝒢] is a 𝒢-measurable integrable random variable Z satisfying ∫ₐZ dP=∫ₐX dP for every A∈𝒢. It exists and is unique up to almost sure equality.

03

Finite partition

If 𝒢 is generated by a finite partition with positive-probability cells Aᵢ, then E[X|𝒢] equals E[X1ₐᵢ]/P(Aᵢ) on each cell.

Key theorems

01

Tower property

If ℋ⊆𝒢⊆ℱ and X is integrable, E[E[X|𝒢]|ℋ]=E[X|ℋ] almost surely. In particular, taking total expectations recovers E[X].

02

Taking out known factors

For bounded 𝒢-measurable Y and integrable X, E[YX|𝒢]=Y E[X|𝒢] almost surely.

03

Least-squares projection

If X∈L², then E[X|𝒢] uniquely minimizes E[(X−Z)²] among 𝒢-measurable Z∈L², up to almost sure equality. The residual is orthogonal to every such Z.

Notable applications

01

Prediction

E[X|Y] is the best squared-error predictor of X from Y. Ordinary linear regression restricts predictions to linear combinations of features; the conditional mean need not be linear.

02

Variance decomposition

For X∈L², Var(X)=E[Var(X|𝒢)]+Var(E[X|𝒢]). This separates uncertainty remaining after observation from variation explained by it.

Watch the assumptions

  • E[X|𝒢] is generally a random variable, not a single number.
  • The tower property requires nested information. Conditioning on an event of probability zero cannot use the elementary ratio formula.

Work it out

Try before revealing

01 / Compute

A fair die gives X. You learn only whether X is odd. Find E[X|parity] and verify its expectation.

Hint

Average separately on {1,3,5} and {2,4,6}.

Solution

The conditional mean is 3 on odd outcomes and 4 on even outcomes. Its expectation is (3+4)/2=3.5=E[X].

02 / Prove

Write m=E[X|𝒢] for X∈L². Show E[(X−Z)²]=E[(X−m)²]+E[(m−Z)²] for any 𝒢-measurable Z∈L².

Hint

Expand the square and use residual orthogonality.

Solution

Write X−Z=(X−m)+(m−Z). The cross term has expectation zero because m−Z is 𝒢-measurable and X−m is orthogonal to L²(𝒢). The two remaining squares give the identity, proving minimality and uniqueness up to almost sure equality.
