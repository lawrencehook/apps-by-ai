Change ringing

In an English bell tower, each ringer takes a rope and controls one swinging bell. The band begins by sounding the bells from highest to lowest, an order called rounds. Each bell keeps its pitch. By adjusting the timing of a swing, two neighboring ringers can exchange places in the next round of strikes.

This practice probably began in England in the late sixteenth or early seventeenth century. By 1668, ringers could learn the patterns from a book. Tintinnalogia, published in London that year, set out how four ringers could sound all 24 possible orders and come back to rounds. The first study below plays the sequence printed in that book.
