# Interactive projects from historical archives

The Bach player works because a score preserves relationships a listener can explore. Muting one voice lets you hear what another voice contributes, and the printed score gives you a way to check what you heard. These projects look for a similar opportunity in weaving drafts, machine drawings, photographic plates, and dance notation.

## Weaving drafts from 1817

Start with a bird’s-eye pattern from the Bronsons’ weaving handbook. Place the draft beside a working loom. Press a treadle to raise a set of threads, then pass the shuttle through the opening. Up close, each pass adds a single row. Zoom out after several repeats and the diamonds appear. Select a diamond to trace one of its threads back through the loom to the marks on the page. Change one treadle in the sequence and see where the pattern breaks.

The handbook has more than thirty numbered drafts. A working loom that can interpret one draft gives the collection a common foundation, much as the Bach player serves all fifteen Inventions. [The Domestic Manufacturer’s Assistant, 1817](https://www.handweaving.net/textile-documents/8421/the-domestic-manufacturer-s-assistant-and-family-directory-in-the-arts-of.html)

## A cabinet of mechanical linkages

Cornell’s 1882 Peaucellier model poses a visual puzzle. Turn a crank in a circle and a pencil attached to another part of the linkage traces an exact straight line. Put the photograph beside a model with the same bars and pivots. Let the visitor drag the crank, then move the pencil to another joint to discover the curve it traces. Cornell identifies 39 related straight-line mechanisms in its collection, enough to compare how different builders solved the same problem. [Cornell’s Reuleaux collection · Peaucellier straight-line mechanism](https://digital.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss%3A372718)

### From a hand crank to a walk-through engine

The larger ambition is to walk around inside a running car engine. Give the visitor control of the crankshaft as well as the camera. Standing beside a valve, turn the shaft five degrees until the valve just begins to lift, then reverse those five degrees. Pull back and follow the chain of parts that made it move.

Boston’s Deer Island steam pumping engine offers a documented subject for a first reconstruction. Its engineering record identifies photographs of the high-, intermediate-, and low-pressure pistons and describes how eccentrics controlled the valves. Begin with one valve and its drive, and check the available views and dimensions before modeling the whole engine. [Library of Congress · Deer Island Pumping Station engineering record](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ma/ma1400/ma1439/data/ma1439data.pdf)

## A tide predictor with a hand crank

William Thomson’s 1872 machine used ten rotating components and a cord running over pulleys to add their motions and trace a predicted tide. Build the first exhibit around one harbor and one month. Turn the crank with one pulley engaged to draw a regular wave. Engage a second and watch successive high tides grow and shrink as the cycles drift in and out of step. Keep the cord and the pen in view together so the visitor can follow the addition taking place. [Science Museum Group · Thomson’s tide predictor](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co53901)

## A horse’s stride in three views

Which feet are holding the horse up? For plate 579, Muybridge described twelve successive exposures from each of three viewpoints, spaced 0.126 seconds apart, covering one walking stride. Scrub through the photographs with four hoofprints underneath that light up on ground contact. Switch viewpoints while holding the instant fixed to inspect a hoof obscured in one view. A later version could add an orbitable reconstruction of the skeleton, with the source frames beside it so visitors can judge the fit. [Muybridge’s description of plate 579](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/muybridge-descriptive-zoopraxography.pdf) · [Penn’s account of the camera setup](https://archives.upenn.edu/exhibits/penn-history/muybridge/)

## Two court duets from 1702

Pécour’s duets survive in Feuillet notation. The lines trace the dancers’ paths across the floor, signs on either side specify steps, and bar lines on the paths correspond to bars in the music. Lay the original page flat as a ballroom floor. As the score plays, two pairs of footprints travel along the ink. Pause where the dancers pass each other and look at how their paths and the musical phrase meet. This could sit beside the Bach player and use the same measure-by-measure navigation. [Library of Congress · Pécour’s two duets](https://www.loc.gov/item/44029074/)

## Where to begin

The Peaucellier linkage is the smallest prototype. One crank, one pencil, and one complete revolution are enough to see whether the interaction makes the geometry intelligible. Weaving has the strongest path to a sustained catalog. A second draft would test whether the same loom can reveal how two patterns differ.
